# It Was Magical
*Main Street at Christmas, Kendallville*

It was for anybody who went uptown in December. You walked with your family, Christmas shopping, from one store to the next. There were so many people out. You saw friends. People would smile and say hello, and friends driving by honked and waved. Ask what it was like, and the word that keeps coming back is the same plain word: magical. It really was.

And over all of it, there was the music.

They used to play Christmas music uptown. Carols came over the loudspeakers, and because back then there were actual shops to shop at, you heard it as you moved from one shop to another. There were large speakers on a building uptown. It added to the Christmas spirit, and there was so much Christmas spirit to add to.

The song that stays is Silver Bells. Snow and shops and Silver Bells. If you were lucky it was snowing, and you walked in the snow beside your mother, going Christmas shopping, with Silver Bells playing on Main Street, and if it is still your favorite Christmas song, that is probably why. You miss her still.

Back then you did not see Christmas decorations, or Christmas movies, until after Thanksgiving. Then the decorations hung from the light posts and across Main Street. The stores were beautiful. They were well kept and open business hours, and later on Friday nights, if memory serves. Every store was filled with love, and the customer was always right.

There was the Christmas parade, and it was so exciting to watch.

And there was Penney's. Near Christmas, Penney's opened its basement, and it was a toy wonderland. You may think you saw Santa down there too. It was magical, mostly because Christmastime was the only time you got to see it, and then you went home and spent hours studying the toys in the catalog. And at Christmas, a big Santa Mouse decorated the window.

Down by Bixler Lake there was the house that had Christmas every year, George Workman's Santa's Workshop, a staple in a little town; but that is the lake's story to tell.

No matter your mood when you arrived uptown, it was always better when you left.

Those days are gone now. They are gone forever, and it is sad. More history gone. So much has been lost in Kendallville.

More recently the old school building, the one there was once talk of tearing down, went up in Christmas lights, with a Santa sleigh on it, and people drove by at night and in the morning to look. It may be the best Christmas lights in Kendallville now, and it was welcome, especially since the downtown was dark. If you were in eighth or ninth grade in that building around 1970, it sure did not look like that. That sleigh sent some of the town further back still, to Wolf and Dessauer in downtown Fort Wayne, many years ago, the lighted Santa and his sleigh on one side of the store and the huge lighted wreath on the other, the display you went to see every year.

You can still walk Main Street in December. What you cannot do is walk it the old way: beside your mother, in the snow, with Silver Bells coming from the big speakers uptown, from one store to the next, through a crowd where people would smile and say hello. It was magical. It really was.
