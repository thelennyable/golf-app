# The Black Diamond Steak
*Shook's*

Every town keeps one place for the nights that matter. The place you went on your birthday. The place a boy took you when he got paid. In Kendallville that place was Shook's, and the town has never stopped missing it.

It was always the special place. You might go there for prom, and come back years later for a rehearsal dinner in the back room. You might go only a handful of times in your life and remember every one of them, because every one of them was an occasion. You might have met your husband there. It was classy, and you felt right at home at the same time, which is a harder trick than it sounds.

You came in through the lobby, and the salad bar was dead ahead.

That salad bar was the best around. And then there was the cheese. The cheese ball. The cheese spread, with the garlic toast. You have yet to find anything like it. There was popcorn, too, and you dipped it in sour cream.

Then there was the fish. Shook's had the best fish in town, and nobody from Kendallville needs to be told that twice. There was broasted chicken, breaded tenderloins, fish and chicken family meals. There was ham and beans, and the big sandwich, and broiled shrimp in butter on your birthday.

And then there was the steak.

Ask about Shook's and before long you are hearing about the Black Diamond. Of everything on the menu, it is the thing the town has not let go of. It was the favorite steak, the best steak ever, notoriously famous, the steak you knew about even if you were not a steak eater. It was a marinated steak, and to some the marinade tasted like teriyaki. You could have it smothered in mushrooms and onions. On a Friday night you could have a Black Diamond, a baked potato, a salad, and a couple of draft beers.

The steaks came in already marinated. Where from, nobody agrees, and it hardly matters, because the marinade has turned into a kind of town puzzle. You can come close. It is still not the same. It is just better when you go out, and Shook's is not there to go out to.

There was a sirloin for two, back when the place first opened, and the big sirloins came from Blue's market. You ate them with salt and pepper, and there is still an argument about whether dill belonged anywhere near them.

The room had its own furniture of memory. Over the fireplace hung a framed piece of electronic artwork, a boat coming into a bay. And there was the bar, and for a while Mickey Mantle came to Shook's and sat at it. There are signed baseballs and photographs from those days. If you missed getting his autograph, you are still sick about it.

But the reason the place felt the way it did had two names, and the names were Bob and Mary Lou. The same Shooks who had the Dairy Queen. It was a family-run business, and you were always greeted with a smile. The servers were some of the best of any restaurant around. Bob was a real interesting guy to talk to, and Mrs. Shook was always so pleasant and nice. Great family owners, great family restaurant, nothing like it. The Shook family welcomed everyone, and that, more than any steak, is what the town is describing when it talks about Shook's.

It was a first job for kids from the high school, busing tables, washing dishes, working the kitchen. If you worked there, it may still be your favorite job. After closing there was a backboard and a basket on the back of the building. You could put in ten years there and call them the best bosses you ever had. If you worked for them, you might still wonder whether Bob and Mary Lou ever knew how many people they touched and helped along the way.

Between Shook's in Kendallville and St. James over in Avilla, you never needed to drive to another town for a good dinner.

Now you do. The place sold, and the building came down, and a gas station stands where it was. Bring up Shook's and the town answers with one sentence, in one wording or another: Kendallville needs a restaurant like that. Not a particular cuisine. Just a nice sit-down place with a little class and good food.

What the town misses is not only a steak or a cheese ball or a plate of fish. It is the version of itself that walked into the lobby on a special night, saw the salad bar dead ahead, and was greeted with a smile.
