# Walk-In Sauce
*Jim's Pizza*

Jim's Pizza belonged to Friday night. Your parents called it in, or you did, from a number you still have memorized, and after enough Fridays the voice on the other end knew what you wanted before you got past your name. Maybe your house had it on Saturday night after church instead, or on Sunday night with chocolate shakes made in the blender. It was supper and it was the treat, both at once. If Jim's Pizza does not show up in your blood test, you might as well be from another country.

It came from Wayne Street, and a good part of Kendallville grew up within reach of it. You could live a few blocks away on Wood Street, or down the street, or next door, or across the street for thirty years, and the smell of that pizza was simply part of the neighborhood.

## Where it came from

Jim Wigginton started it in the early sixties, in the kitchen of Chuck's Tavern, and in those days Jim would deliver. Then he and Gwen opened Jim's Pizza on Wayne Street. It may have been the first pizza you ever tasted. After the Wiggintons came John and Ruth, and after them Dave and Linda Grubb. Dave had worked the produce department at Harman's IGA, and there he was always Grubby, so thoroughly that you could forget his name was Dave. Linda answered the phone, and she knew your voice. Now it is Mark and Wendy.

For a lot of Kendallville kids it was also a job. You could start out washing dishes for about twenty dollars a week. You could work the window. If your first job was there in 1971, a large pizza was $4.25. The best part of the shift was the pizza you got to eat on break.

## The order

Pepperoni, first and always, for more people than anything else. After that it gets personal. Pepperoni and mushroom. Sausage and mushroom. Hamburger and onion. Ham and pepperoni. Pepperoni and green olives. Loaded. Orders that come out as a string of letters, CSMO, PMS, CSSMOPep, and mean something exact to the person on the phone.

And then the instructions. Well done, which some people take all the way to burnt. Light on the sauce. Extra sauce. Extra cheese. Thick crust. Double crust, which means the bottom crust doubled up, and which you may have ordered that way your whole life because your father did. Crispers in the crust, to give the middle pieces some crunch. A ham base. No top sauce. You can grow up in Kendallville eating Jim's and learn about half of these as a grown adult, which is its own small shock, like finding out the house you grew up in had a room you never saw.

There is the Grubby Special, which carries Grubby's name, and which prompts more questions than any other pizza on Wayne Street. What is on a Grubby Special? The answers come back with a lot of the word double in them: double pepperoni, double sausage, double mushroom, double cheese, extra sauce, a thicker crust. Nobody's list is quite the same as the next one. It is still the one people tell you that you have to try.

And there is the walk-in sauce. It is the pizza sauce before it gets thinned out with water, kept in the walk-in cooler, which is where the name comes from. It is thicker, with more spice to it and a little kick. You ask for it, and you can have it on the top or the bottom or both. Plenty of people have been ordering it for years as walking sauce, and it arrives just the same. You can eat Jim's Pizza for forty years and never hear of it.

## The grease

That is not grease. It is pepperoni juice, or pepperoni sweat, and it is enough sauce for anybody. The thick crust is there to soak it up, and the double crust keeps it from soaking the cardboard. Walk-in sauce on top and bottom is heartburn city, and worth it, and if you feel it going down you know it is good. Jim's goes down with an Alka-Seltzer chaser and a glass of water on the nightstand.

Not everybody. There are people in Kendallville who never cared for it.

The next morning, if any of it made it to the refrigerator, you ate it cold, peeled carefully off the cardboard. Cold Jim's for breakfast. There is no wrong way to eat a Jim's.

## Carried away

Then you moved. To Fort Wayne, to Indianapolis, to Alabama, to Alaska, to Charlotte, and there turned out to be nothing anywhere quite like it. Coming home might mean bringing back a large cooler and filling it with as many frozen pizzas as it would hold. It might mean six or ten of them for the flight to Alaska, cut into quarters with the cardboard still on, each wrapped on its own, still frozen when you landed. It might mean driving up from Fort Wayne and taking home two extra. If you have not had one since you left in 1987, you have thought about it more than once. You would, if you are honest, kill for one.

## Still on Wayne Street

Jim's Pizza is still on Wayne Street, and Mark and Wendy will take your order. You can call the number you never forgot and get it exactly the way your father always ordered it. The pizza is still there. It is the kid waiting on it on a Friday night who is gone.
